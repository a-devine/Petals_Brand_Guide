Proportion

Linen carries the page. Petal Pink and Mauve Rose share the floral work. Warm Charcoal handles type. Sage and Gold appear as accents only — a sprig, a ribbon, a flourish.

Pairings

Mauve on Linen for headlines. Charcoal on Linen for body. Linen on Charcoal for hospitality moments. Gold reserved for celebration.

Print & Web

CMYK builds live in the swatch sheet. Web tones use the hex above; ensure WCAG-AA contrast on all body copy by pairing Charcoal on Linen.

§ 04

The Voice

Warm, observant, never precious. We speak the way we'd hand someone a stem across a garden table.

Sound like this

Joy in every bouquet. Purpose in every petal.

  • Lead with the gesture, not the product.
  • Name the flower, name the season, name the maker.
  • Use specifics — a Saturday, a kitchen window, a hand-tied stem.
  • Let the sentence breathe; one short line is plenty.
  • Always credit the community partner when relevant.

Avoid this

"Premium florals delivered fast."

  • Marketing superlatives — best, ultimate, premium, luxury.
  • Stock floral metaphors that belong on a greeting card.
  • Long sentences with stacked clauses and em-dashes inside em-dashes.
  • Hard-sell calls to action. We invite, we don't push.
  • Emoji in body copy (a single ❀ or 🌷 in a tagline is the limit).
The Tagline
Joy in every bouquet. Purpose in every petal.
